Choreographing Wayfinding in the Healthcare Design Process

Mojtaba Ashour, Qi Yang, Zhiwen Qiu, and Saleh Kalantari

  • Navigation
  • Hospital
  • Design Management
  • Architectural Practice
  • Spatial Orientation
Under review

While wayfinding strategies have been extensively studied, less is known about how wayfinding is integrated into healthcare design workflows in practice. This project investigates how healthcare architects, planners, and design experts conceive, negotiate, and integrate wayfinding strategies throughout complex healthcare projects. To address this gap, we conducted in-depth focus groups with 34 experienced healthcare design practitioners from leading firms across the United States and Canada. Transcripts were analyzed using reflexive thematic analysis to identify common challenges, practices, and opportunities for improving the integration of wayfinding expertise.

The study identified several persistent challenges, including insufficient early and sustained collaboration among specialists, limited pre- and post-occupancy evaluation, and a lack of flexible and practical guidelines for integrating wayfinding into design workflows. Building on these findings, we developed an industry-informed double-diamond framework emphasizing deep contextual understanding, early decision-making, interdisciplinary collaboration, iterative testing, and systematic evaluation, providing design teams and clients with a practical scaffold. The project contributes a process-focused perspective that complements existing research on wayfinding strategies and highlights the importance of continued collaboration between academia and industry to develop more effective, evidence-informed, and human-centered approaches.
