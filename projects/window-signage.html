Effects of Window and Signage Presence on Wayfinding Route Choices in a Healthcare Setting

Mojtaba Ashour, Qi Yang, Armin Mostafavi, Robin Snell, Jialin Wu, Yifei Gao, Wenqian Niu, and Saleh Kalantari

  • Spatial Schema
  • Wayfinding Heuristics
  • Pathfinding
  • Environmental Cognition
Under review

Wayfinding in healthcare environments is often supported through explicit cues such as signage, maps, and color-coding. However, architectural features embedded within the environment may also shape how people interpret and navigate space. This project investigates how an implicit architectural cue, the presence of a window, interacts with an explicit cue, signage, to influence route choice behavior in a real-world hospital environment. In collaboration with Parkin Architects, we conducted a within-subject experiment with 68 participants completing four wayfinding tasks and analyzed route choices using generalized linear mixed-effects models.

The findings identified a strong effect of window presence. Contrary to the common assumption that windows naturally attract people toward a route, participants were less likely to choose a straight-ahead route when a window was positioned at its end. This finding suggests that, depending on its spatial configuration and environmental context, a window may be interpreted not as an attractor but as a boundary or termination of navigable space, highlighting how architectural features can influence wayfinding through the meanings and expectations they evoke, rather than solely through their physical properties. Our findings call for a more nuanced understanding of architectural cues in healthcare design and further research into how environmental context shapes the way people interpret and navigate space.
