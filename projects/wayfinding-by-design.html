03

Integrating Spatial Cognition Research, Environmental Psychology, and Expert Practice

Mojtaba Ashour, Qi Yang, Zhiwen Qiu, and Saleh Kalantari

  • Wayfinding
  • Healthcare Design
  • Spatial Cognition
  • Affective State
  • Environmental Psychology
Under review

Wayfinding research draws on various fields including spatial cognition, environmental psychology, and architectural design, yet these perspectives are often studied separately. This project explores how established theories of wayfinding align with the knowledge and reasoning used by design practitioners working in complex healthcare environments. We examined how professionals understand and apply concepts related to spatial cognition, environmental perception, and navigation in real-world design practice.

The findings revealed strong connections between professional design reasoning and established cognitive concepts such as cognitive mapping, uncertainty reduction, and schema-based perception. At the same time, practitioners emphasized factors that are less prominent in traditional cognition-centered models, including affective states, environmental affordances, and the meanings people associate with places. Building on these insights, we propose a conceptual framework that understands wayfinding as a dynamic interaction among cognitive, affective, and environmental factors, shaped by contextual conditions and individual differences. The project contributes a more ecologically grounded perspective on wayfinding and demonstrates how expert design practice can serve as a valuable source for extending theory and strengthening connections between research and healthcare design.
