About me

I am a design researcher focusing on human-building interaction (HBI). I see people and buildings as being in continuous exchange, where cognition (mind), affect (mood), and the built environment (milieu) shape one another as people move through, interpret, and make sense of space. I think of this as a dynamic interplay between our internal and external worlds. My aim is to advance a theoretically grounded understanding of HBI as it unfolds in situ, and to translate that knowledge into actionable insight for design practice.

Research projects

I work at both ends of the continuum between theory building and design translation, using experimental, psychophysiological, qualitative, and observational methods, and collaborating closely with architects and planners.

Effects of Window and Signage Presence on Wayfinding Route Choices in a Healthcare Setting

This project investigates how an implicit architectural cue, the presence of a window, interacts with an explicit cue, signage, to influence route choice behavior in a real-world hospital environment.

02

Design Strategies for Enhancing Wayfinding in Complex Healthcare Environments

This project investigates how healthcare designers conceptualize and implement strategies that support effective wayfinding in complex hospital environments, drawing on focus groups with 34 experienced designers.

03

Integrating Spatial Cognition Research, Environmental Psychology, and Expert Practice

Wayfinding research draws on spatial cognition, environmental psychology, and architectural design, yet these perspectives are often studied separately. This project explores how established theories of wayfinding align with the knowledge and reasoning used by designers of complex healthcare environments.

04

Real Enough for What? A Review of Human Response Correspondence Between VR and Real Environments

A systematic review of 65 empirical studies directly comparing matched virtual and real indoor environments across perception, cognition, affect, and behavior and interaction.

05

Choreographing Wayfinding in the Healthcare Design Process

This project investigates how healthcare architects, planners, and design experts conceive, negotiate, and integrate wayfinding strategies throughout complex healthcare projects.

06

Task Structure Shapes Dyadic Interaction in M-VR for Older Adults with Differing Cognitive Abilities

Part of SERVE, an NIH-funded VR intervention for older adults with mild cognitive impairment, this project studies how task structure and environmental affordances shape social interaction when older adults with MCI are paired with cognitively healthy volunteers in multiplayer VR.
